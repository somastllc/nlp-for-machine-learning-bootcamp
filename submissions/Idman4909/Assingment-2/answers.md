# Part A – Research Questions

### 1. What is text preprocessing?

Text preprocessing is the process of preparing raw text before it is used by an NLP system. It can include cleaning, normalization, tokenization, stopword removal, stemming, and lemmatization. Preprocessing helps reduce noise and makes text easier for a model to analyze.

However, excessive cleaning can remove useful information. For example, removing the word "not" from "I am not happy" changes the meaning and may cause a sentiment classifier to predict the wrong result.

### 2. Text normalization

Normalization converts text into a consistent form. Common methods include lowercasing, removing extra whitespace, and applying Unicode normalization.

Lowercasing is useful when "Apple" and "apple" should be treated as the same word, such as in ordinary topic classification. It can be harmful in named-entity recognition because "US" and "us" have different meanings. Therefore, normalization should depend on the NLP task.

### 3. Tokenization

Tokenization divides text into smaller units called tokens. Word tokenization splits text into words, subword tokenization divides uncommon words into familiar pieces, and character tokenization splits text into individual characters.

Word tokenization is easy to interpret but may create a large vocabulary and struggle with unknown words. Subword tokenization handles rare and new words more effectively, while character tokenization can handle spelling variation but produces longer sequences.

### 4. Stopwords

Stopwords are frequent words such as "the", "is", and "and". Removing them can reduce the number of tokens and help some information-retrieval or topic-analysis tasks.

However, stopword removal can damage meaning in sentiment analysis, question answering, and other tasks. Words such as "not", "no", and "never" are especially important. Stopword lists should also be adapted to the language and task; an English stopword list should not automatically be used for Somali text.

### 5. Stemming and lemmatization

Stemming reduces a word by removing prefixes or suffixes using rules. It may produce a root that is not a valid dictionary word. Porter, Lancaster, and Snowball are examples of stemming algorithms.

Lemmatization converts a word to its dictionary base form (lemma), using vocabulary and often grammatical information. For example, the lemma of "running" is "run", while the lemma of "better" may be "good" when it is used as an adjective. Lemmatization is usually more linguistically accurate but can require more resources.

### 6. Regular expressions

Regular expressions (regex) are patterns used to find, remove, or replace text. For example, `\d+` matches one or more digits, and `[^a-z\s]` matches characters other than lowercase English letters and whitespace.

Regex can remove unwanted punctuation or replace phone numbers with a placeholder. However, broad patterns can delete important information, such as punctuation in dates, decimal numbers, URLs, or apostrophes in contractions.

### 7. Case study: choosing preprocessing for sentiment analysis

For sentiment analysis, preprocessing should preserve words and symbols that express opinion. Lowercasing and whitespace normalization can make text more consistent. Tokenization is required to split the text into units.

Stopword removal should be used carefully because negations such as "not" and "never" can reverse sentiment. Stemming or lemmatization may group related word forms, but the effect should be tested. A suitable pipeline is one that removes noise while preserving sentiment-bearing information.

### Short response – Preprocessing for a spam detector

A spam detector should normalize whitespace and tokenize messages consistently. It can use regex to replace phone numbers and money amounts with placeholders because these patterns may be useful signals for spam.

It should not remove every number or punctuation mark automatically, since repeated exclamation marks and currency amounts may help identify spam. The preprocessing pipeline should preserve useful signals and be evaluated using real validation data.
