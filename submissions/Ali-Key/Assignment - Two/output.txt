NLP PRACTICAL — REPORT - TEXT PREPROCESSING

======================================================================
Purpose
This report explains common text-preprocessing techniques using simple examples. 
It also shows why preprocessing choices depend on the NLP task.
======================================================================

======================================================================
1) LOWERCASE — T1
======================================================================

Before:
'URGENT!!! You have WON a £1,000 gift card. Call 0712345678 NOW!'

After:
'urgent!!! you have won a £1,000 gift card. call 0712345678 now!'

Explanation:
Lowercasing changes capital letters into small letters.

Example:
'URGENT' -> 'urgent'
'WON'    -> 'won'

Why?
It helps treat 'URGENT' and 'urgent' as the same word.

Important:
For spam detection, capital letters can also be useful information.
For example, 'URGENT!!!' may be a strong spam signal.

Conclusion:
Lowercasing is useful, but we should use it carefully.


======================================================================
2) WHITESPACE NORMALIZATION — T4
======================================================================

Before:
'   The   delivery   was   fast,   and the  packaging   was   great.'

After:
'The delivery was fast, and the packaging was great.'

Explanation:
Whitespace normalization removes extra spaces.

Why?
Extra spaces usually do not add meaning and can make text messy.

Conclusion:
Whitespace normalization makes text cleaner and more consistent.


======================================================================
3) REGEX CLEANING
======================================================================

Before:
'urgent!!! you have won a £1,000 gift card. call 0712345678 now!'

Regex:
[^a-z\s]

After:
'urgent you have won a  gift card call  now'

Explanation:
This regex keeps only:
- lowercase letters (a-z)
- spaces (\s)

It removes:
- punctuation
- numbers
- currency symbols
- phone numbers

Example:

'!!!'       -> removed
'£1,000'    -> removed
'0712345678' -> removed

Problem:
Some of this information may be useful.

For example:
- £1,000 can be a spam signal
- 0712345678 is a phone number
- !!! can show strong or suspicious language

----------------------------------------------------------------------
BETTER APPROACH: REPLACE IMPORTANT INFORMATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Instead of deleting important information, we can replace it.

Phone number:
0712345678 -> PHONE_NUMBER

Money:
£1,000 -> MONEY

Repeated punctuation:
!!! -> !

After replacement:
'urgent! you have won a MONEY gift card. call PHONE_NUMBER now!'

Conclusion:
Regex cleaning should depend on the NLP task.

Do not remove useful information without a reason.


======================================================================
4) TOKENIZATION
======================================================================

Text:
"I don't think this jacket is worth the price. Not happy at all 😞"

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Using split()
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Tokens:
['I', "don't", 'think', 'this', 'jacket', 'is', 'worth', 'the',
 'price.', 'Not', 'happy', 'at', 'all', '😞']

Token count:
14

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Using word_tokenize()
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Tokens:
['I', 'do', "n't", 'think', 'this', 'jacket', 'is', 'worth', 'the',
 'price', '.', 'Not', 'happy', 'at', 'all', '😞']

Token count:
16

Explanation:
Tokenization means breaking text into smaller pieces called tokens.

For example:

"don't" -> "do" + "n't"

"price." -> "price" + "."

Why?
Machine Learning models usually need text to be converted into
smaller units before processing it.

Conclusion:
Tokenization is one of the basic steps in NLP.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
T4 TOKENIZATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Text:
'The delivery was fast, and the packaging was great.'

Tokens:
['The', 'delivery', 'was', 'fast', ',', 'and', 'the',
 'packaging', 'was', 'great', '.']

Token count:
11


----------------------------------------------------------------------
T4 VOCABULARY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Vocabulary:
['and', 'delivery', 'fast', 'great', 'packaging', 'the', 'was']

Vocabulary size:
7

Explanation:
Vocabulary means the unique words/tokens in the text.

For example:
'the' appears more than once, but it is counted only once
in the vocabulary.

Conclusion:
Vocabulary = unique tokens.


======================================================================
5) STOPWORDS AND NEGATION
======================================================================

T2:
"I don't think this jacket is worth the price. Not happy at all 😞"

Tokens:
['I', 'do', "n't", 'think', 'this', 'jacket', 'is', 'worth',
 'the', 'price', '.', 'Not', 'happy', 'at', 'all', '😞']

----------------------------------------------------------------------
STOPWORD REMOVAL
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Result:
["n't", 'think', 'jacket', 'worth', 'price', '.', 'happy', '😞']

Explanation:
Stopwords are common words that are often removed because they may
not provide much information for some NLP tasks.

Examples:
- the
- is
- at
- this

But we must be careful with NEGATION.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
NEGATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Important negation words:

- not
- no
- never
- n't

Example:

'happy'
and
'not happy'

have different meanings.

If we remove 'not':

'not happy' -> 'happy'

The meaning becomes wrong.

Conclusion:
For sentiment analysis and complaint detection, it is usually
better to keep important negation words.


======================================================================
6) STEMMING
======================================================================

Explanation:
Stemming reduces words to a shorter common form.

Example:

connection  -> connect
connected   -> connect
connecting  -> connect

This helps a model recognize that these words are related.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
STEMMING COMPARISON
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Word           Porter       Lancaster      Snowball       Regexp
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
connection     connect      connect        connect        connect
connected      connect      connect        connect        connect
connecting     connect      connect        connect        connect
organization   organ        org            organ          organiz
organized      organ        org            organ          organiz
university     univers      univers        univers        university
universal      univers      univers        univers        universal
flies          fli          fli            fli            flie
wolves         wolv         wolv           wolv           wolve
better         better       bet            better         better
ran            ran          ran            ran            ran

Explanation:
Different stemmers use different rules.

Some stemmers are more aggressive than others.

For example:

university -> univers

'univers' is not a normal English word, but stemming can still
produce it.

Conclusion:
Stemming is fast and simple, but the result may not always be
a real English word.


======================================================================
7) LEMMATIZATION
======================================================================

Explanation:
Lemmatization changes a word into its correct dictionary/base form.

Examples:

running -> run
studies -> study
better -> good
ran -> run

Lemmatization usually gives a more meaningful result than stemming.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
EXAMPLES
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Word           Lemma
----------------------------
running        run
studies        study
flies          fly
wolves         wolf
better         good
ran            run

----------------------------------------------------------------------
STEMMING vs LEMMATIZATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Stemming:
'studies' -> 'studi'

Lemmatization:
'studies' -> 'study'

Explanation:
Stemming simply cuts or reduces a word.

Lemmatization uses language information to find the correct base word.

Conclusion:
Lemmatization is usually more linguistically accurate, but it can
require more processing.


======================================================================
8) SENTENCE TOKENIZATION
======================================================================

Text:
'Mr. Smith arrived in London on Friday. He met the Apple team at
9 a.m.'

Sentences:

1 -> Mr. Smith arrived in London on Friday.

2 -> He met the Apple team at 9 a.m.

Sentence count:
2

Explanation:
Sentence tokenization separates a paragraph into individual
sentences.

The tokenizer should understand that:

'Mr.'
and
'a.m.'

are abbreviations and should not normally end a sentence.

Conclusion:
Sentence tokenization helps us process text sentence by sentence.


======================================================================
9) POS TAGGING
======================================================================

POS means:
Part of Speech

It tells us the grammatical role of a word.

Text:
'Mr. Smith arrived in London on Friday.'

----------------------------------------------------------------------
POS TAGS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Mr.        -> NNP
Smith      -> NNP
arrived    -> VBD
in         -> IN
London     -> NNP
on         -> IN
Friday     -> NNP

----------------------------------------------------------------------
COMMON POS TAGS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

NNP -> Proper noun
NN  -> Noun
VBD -> Past-tense verb
IN  -> Preposition
PRP -> Personal pronoun
DT  -> Determiner
CD  -> Number

Example:

'London' -> NNP
'arrived' -> VBD
'He' -> PRP

Conclusion:
POS tagging helps the computer understand the grammatical role
of each word.


======================================================================
10) NAMED ENTITY RECOGNITION — NER
======================================================================

NER means:
Named Entity Recognition

NER identifies important real-world information in text.

Example:

'Mr. Smith arrived in London. He met the Apple team.'

Expected entities:

PERSON:
Mr. Smith

LOCATION / GPE:
London

ORGANIZATION:
Apple

Explanation:
NER can identify:

PERSON        -> people
GPE           -> geographical locations
ORGANIZATION  -> companies or organizations

Important:
NER is not always perfect.

A model may identify 'Mr.' and 'Smith' separately instead of
recognizing them as one person.

It may also fail to recognize 'Apple' as an organization.

Conclusion:
NER results should always be checked.

======================================================================
FINAL SUMMARY
======================================================================

NLP preprocessing prepares raw text for analysis and Machine Learning.

The main techniques are:

1. Lowercasing
   -> Converts uppercase letters to lowercase.

2. Whitespace normalization
   -> Removes unnecessary spaces.

3. Regex cleaning
   -> Removes or replaces unwanted characters.

4. Tokenization
   -> Breaks text into smaller tokens.

5. Stopword removal
   -> Removes selected common words.

6. Stemming
   -> Reduces words to a shorter root-like form.

7. Lemmatization
   -> Converts words to their dictionary/base form.

8. Sentence tokenization
   -> Separates text into sentences.

9. POS tagging
   -> Identifies the grammatical role of words.

10. NER
    -> Identifies people, places, organizations, and other entities.
